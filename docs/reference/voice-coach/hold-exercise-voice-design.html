02 The core idea

ELI5 — why a hold is a different animal from reps

Reps and holds look similar on screen but are opposite shapes to a voice coach. Get this one idea and the rest of the doc is obvious.

Reps = a string of eventsEach rep is a discrete thing with a start and an end. The coach has natural moments to speak: "a rep just landed" → count it; "that rep was clean/bad" → praise/correct. Events everywhere.
Hold = one long eventA 30s plank is a single continuous thing. There are no rep boundaries to hang cues on. The only real "events" are state changes: you got into position, you broke form, you fixed it, you finished.

The clock is "earned time" — but earned means in-pose, not flawless RULED 07-11

This is the fact everything hinges on, and it's where Nam reversed the original proposal. The hold clock advances while you are recognisably in the pose — it stops only when you leave it. It does not freeze on a form fault. The shipped code today does the opposite (it freezes on any sag), and that's exactly the line the migration changes:

OUTER ring — anti-cheat (gates the clock)"Are you still doing a plank at all?" On the floor, not stood up, not collapsed flat, in frame, not planking on a wall. Lose the outer ring → the clock stops earning. This is the only thing that pauses time.
INNER ring — form quality (clock keeps running)Sagging / piked / elbow within a still- recognisable plank. Coached in real time on the fault channel — but the seconds keep banking. The per-metric fault-seconds still accrue, so the post-set summary stays honest.

💡 Why the reversal — coach, not referee

Users come to Vika to be coached on their faults. Freezing the clock the instant a beginner's hips sag punishes the exact thing they showed up to fix — it reads as a referee blowing a whistle, not a coach. So a fault gets a real-time correction while the clock keeps ticking; only cheating (leaving the pose) stops earning time. The quality honesty lives in the post-set summary (fault-seconds), the clock lives in the time. Two separate jobs.

Mechanically the clock is still "earned time" that only accrues in the holding state — that part is unchanged:

🧒 ELI5 — the one consequence

You cannot schedule còn 15 giây on a wall clock, because leaving the pose stalls earned time. So every time announcement is a crossing of earned in-pose time — "you have now banked 15 seconds." That's a feature, not a workaround: each milestone is a genuine causal event ("you earned this"), which is what the locked deterministic-for-causality principle wants, and it sidesteps no-metronomes — nothing fires on a fixed beat. Under the two-ring ruling the clock almost never stalls mid-hold (only real cheating does it), so those crossings track the real seconds closely.

The trust rule — half of it comes back REFINED 07-12

For reps, the 07-11 ruling was: every landed rep must speak its number (count = registration; a silent rep is ambiguous). Under the 07-12 reps-of-holds model a hold IS a rep — a set is N holds, and each completed hold speaks its number (Một!, Hai!…), deterministic and personality-immune, exactly the rep fleet's count registration. That numeral replaces any hold-specific per-hold completion cue. Inside each hold, the original hold logic still applies:

 Between holds (= the rep layer)Inside a hold (decided)
What silence means Ambiguous — did the hold register? So every completed hold speaks its number (count = registration, verbatim from the rep spec). Reassuring — silence = the clock is running. No news is good news.
What voice marks The completed hold (its number) + the hold's outcome, off its per-hold RepLog. A few sparse milestones (halfway, final stretch — relative to per-hold seconds) + real-time fault corrections. The continuous clean part is deliberately quiet; the end-of-hold countdown is non-verbal earcons, not spoken numbers (§04).

💡 So the whole hold philosophy in one line

Front-load the setup, then stay quiet; coach faults in real time while the clock keeps running, and speak at the meaningful marks (halfway, the final stretch). Leaving the pose is the only thing that stalls the clock. The research below says every discipline that coaches holds converges on the front-load-then-quiet shape.

04 Evidence · sound

The earcon channel — voice carries meaning, sounds carry state

The end-of-hold countdown is non-verbal earcons, not a spoken năm, bốn, ba…. This was Nam's ruling and it is research-backed: every interval timer, boxing-gym clock, and plank app converges on a beep grammar that needs zero teaching.

💡 The channel-separation principle

Voice carries meaning; sounds carry state. A spoken line is for something that needs words — a correction, a milestone, encouragement. The passage of the final seconds is pure state, and a coach never robot-counts it ("a coach says còn 10 giây, not 10, 9, 8"). Encoding the last-3-seconds as identical beeps keeps the two channels from clashing: the voice can still be finishing a cố lên while the beeps tick underneath, because they occupy different perceptual lanes.

The beep grammar (research-verified, needs no teaching)

SignalWhenSoundWhy it reads instantly
Final-3 pipsIn-hold earned seconds T−3, −2, −1 (T = the per-hold target, 07-12) 3 identical short beeps The universal "get ready, almost there" of every interval timer — three even ticks = "three left".
End toneIn-hold earned time hits T — fires once per hold, right as the hold's count numeral lands One distinct, higher/longer tone The "time!" bell — a different sound from the pips, so "done" is unmistakably not "another second".
Continuous tick—none in v1 Research flags an anxiety cost and it clashes with voice. Possible later as an opt-in; not v1.

✔ Why earcons over spoken numerals — and the honest stall

Because the beeps ride earned-second crossings, they stall honestly if the user leaves the pose mid-countdown — which, under the two-ring ruling (§02), almost never happens mid-hold. No spoken countdown means no half-spoken ba… freezing awkwardly. Escape hatch: if the beeps feel worse than numbers on device, swapping to the already-recorded count_1..3 is a one-line content change — flagged in the impl spec, not built.

🔴 Implementation note — earcons must not queue behind voice

The voice sink (voice_sink.dart) is a single FIFO; a beep pushed through it would wait behind a playing 3s milestone line and miss its second. The spec recommends a second, lightweight player running in parallel (channel separation in code, too), fire-and-forget, no hunger/no collision-gap — earcons are deterministic state pips, not outcome cues. See docs/scratch/hold-voice-impl-spec.md §4.

a. Count = the hold's number; time milestones + earcons live inside each hold RULED · refined 07-12

The count (07-12): each completed hold lands as a rep and speaks its number — Một!, Hai!… — the rep fleet's count-registration rule verbatim (deterministic, personality-immune; reuses count_1..N). Inside each hold, the time layer is deterministic, fired on in-hold earned-time crossings, relative to the per-hold target at any duration. For a per-hold target T:

  • Halfway (T/2 in-hold s): Được một nửa rồi — the mandatory milestone every source flags.
  • Final-10 (T−10 in-hold s): còn 10 giây — only when the hold is long enough for it to be distinct from halfway (a short hold keeps halfway only).
  • Final-3 + end = NON-VERBAL earcons: 3 identical short beeps on the hold's last 3 earned seconds, then a distinct end tone at T — landing together with the hold's count numeral on separate channels. The coach never robot-counts (§04). No spoken năm, bốn, ba… in v1.
  • Silent before halfway. The setup intro + Sẵn sàng already confirmed the start; nothing to say until the first mark.

💡 The milestone outcome switch (praise / hustle)

Each milestone fires the time line, and its single outcome slot is decided by measured state since the previous milestone: clean → praise roll (hunger model), may pair (Được một nửa rồi — form đẹp lắm!); struggling (a pose-break or a persistent fault since the last milestone, or the final stretch) → hustle roll (Được một nửa rồi, cố lên!); neither wins its roll → the time line speaks alone. One outcome per moment; never praise two consecutive milestones. This is the hold analog of the rep spec's per-moment exclusivity — the milestone is the moment.

b. The two rings in the state machine RULED — the big change

Today a single isFormBadToDrop condition trips holding → dropping on any fault — sag, pike, bent elbow — and freezes the clock. That is the form-gating the ruling reverses. The migration splits this into two rings:

  • OUTER ring (gates the clock): only a real anti-cheat exit — off the floor / stood up / collapsed flat / out of frame / the wall-plank guard — trips dropping. Thresholds loosened from today's tight values (feel-tune). This break is rare, and its line doubles as the "clock stopped" trust signal.
  • INNER ring (clock keeps running): sag / pike / elbow within a still-recognisable plank no longer touch the state machine. They fire on the criticalFault/softFault channel in real time — identical rules to the rep fleet — while the seconds keep banking.

Here is the single condition being split — the red line currently conflates both rings:

lib/exercise/3.High Plank/high_plank.dart — the drop condition being split (form-gated TODAY, outer-ring-only AFTER)L451–471
// TODAY: one condition, mixing anti-cheat AND form quality —
bool isFormBadToDrop = bodyAngle < DROPPING_PIKE_ANGLE ||     // pike = FORM (inner)
    armAngle < DROPPING_ARM_ANGLE ||                    // bent elbow = FORM (inner)
    kneeAngle < DROPPING_KNEE_ANGLE ||                  // bent knee = FORM (inner)
    hipDev >= DROPPING_SAG_DEVIATION;                    // sag = FORM (inner) <- freezes clock today

if (state == HighPlankState.holding) {
  if (_droppingDebouncer.update(isFormBadToDrop)) {           // AFTER: only OUTER-ring loss reaches here
    _transitionState(HighPlankState.dropping, now);          // clock stops ONLY on a real anti-cheat exit
  }
}
// AFTER: sag/pike/elbow move OUT of this condition into liveFaults (inner ring, clock runs).

So the migration removes the inner-ring terms (sag/pike/elbow) from the drop condition and replaces them with a loosened outer-ring test (recognisable-plank-or-not). Those inner faults instead surface through liveFaults to the criticalFault channel (see §06 R2, §10 audio).

c. Inner-ring faults are coached while the clock runs RULED

A sag / pike / bent elbow within a still-recognisable plank now fires the criticalFault (or softFault) real-time channel with the clock still banking seconds — identical rules to the rep fleet (first occurrence 100% deterministic, collision gap, persistence escalation), with the hold episode as the persistence unit. Fault-seconds keep accruing per metric (sagging_seconds / piked_seconds / elbow_seconds, §02 code) — even better than before, because the clock now runs through the fault, so the honest fault-second count is captured. Post-set summary stays data-honest with no new plumbing.

d. Praise is the clean side of the milestone switch RULED

Praise is one arm of the outcome switch (§05a): eligibility = clean since the previous milestone, a hunger-shaped roll that pairs with the time line when it wins (Được một nửa rồi — form đẹp lắm!). Praise never fires mid-silence — a random compliment 12s into quiet focus startles; milestones are a hold's only structural moments, so that's the only place praise can land. Hard rule 6 becomes "never praise two consecutive milestones."

e. Hustle — milestone-paired (primary) + the re-hold push (secondary) RULED

Two flavours, both quiet-side probability, never deterministic (a guaranteed finish line every set is the rejected metronome):

  • PRIMARY — milestone-paired: the hustle arm of the outcome switch (§05a). When the user is struggling or in the final stretch at a milestone, the slot rolls hustle instead of praise.
  • SECONDARY — the re-hold push (the one causal mid-hold moment): a pose-break in the final third arms hustle; the re-hold commit (dropping → holding) fires it — the user just re-committed under fatigue: Cố lên, sắp xong rồi! This keeps hustle's locked arm-on-behaviour / fire-on-commit shape, re-cast for a continuous hold.

f. Setup / activation countdown / set-complete unchanged

Modality-independent, lifted straight from the rep spec: the per-set intro one-shot, the voiced ba, hai, một activation countdown (3s hold), Sẵn sàng, and common.set_complete. A hold's start position is the plank position, so the 3-second activation hold works exactly as it does for a rep exercise.

g. Completion, and the timeout is DELETED RULED

The original proposal flagged a 90s "give-up" timeout as a data-honesty gap needing its own line. Nam ruled it out entirely: the timeout is deleted as designed behaviour — no 90s give-up ending, no hết giờ line. The migration removes the TIMEOUT_MS path:

g2. Hold structure: the PLANK model — reps-of-holds RE-RULED 07-12

Supersedes the 07-11 "a set = one continuous hold" ruling. Holds adopt the shape Forearm Plank (lib/exercise/plank/plank.dart) already implements: a set contains N HOLDS counted as REPS. Each hold runs a fixed per-hold duration, then a brief internal resting breather (~5s — inside the exercise, NOT the formal rest screen), then the next hold; completing a hold increments repCount and logs a real per-hold RepLog. High Plank migrates to HighPlank(maxHolds, holdSeconds) — hold count from the catalog base_reps, per-hold seconds from base_seconds, no hard-coded per-hold constants (aligning plank's own hard-coded 15s to the catalog is a noted follow-up, out of pilot scope).

  • Why this simplifies the voice design: a hold IS a rep, so count registration, the per-hold outcome, same-fault-once, persistence across holds, and the next-hold reminder all ride the rep fleet's existing machinery — far more directly than the v2 continuous-hold design assumed.
  • The formal multi-set flow sits above this: base_sets sets with the rest screen, each containing the holds. High Plank's catalog row stays interim 3 sets × one 30s hold until the code migration lands, then flips to plank-shaped 1 set × 3 holds × 30s (flip SQL drafted in docs/scratch/hold-catalog-hybrid-fix.sql, Nam applies it).
  • repCount leak fixed by the model: repCount = totalHoldingTimeMs ~/ 1000 is deleted; repCount counts completed holds, within-hold seconds surface only through liveHoldSeconds. UI ring untouched (ruling 9).
  • Verified in plank.dart: the internal hold→rest→hold cycle never leaves the base activated state — re-entering a hold is a debounced pose re-check, NOT a re-run of isInStartPosition — so the setup/activation channel (intro, "một, hai, ba", Sẵn sàng) fires once per set, not per hold (ruling 12 intact).

Details + the open catalog-modality question in the impl spec §5.

h. Out of scope for v1 deferred

  • Yoga / stretch register: same policy, different VoiceScript config — an empty hustle pool + a reduced milestone set. The mechanism already exists; only the behaviour design is deferred.
  • Breathing cues: breathing is not measured, so data honesty forbids a live thở đều cue. If it belongs anywhere, it's baked into the setup-intro recording, not spoken live.

💡 Why the pilot is High Plank only

~20 exercises implement liveHoldSeconds, and they split into two families: effortful isometrics (High / Bear Plank, Superman) — grind, fatigue, needs the halfway push + end earcons — and yoga / stretch poses (Cobra, Butterfly, Warrior 1…) — calm, felt, reduced milestones + empty hustle pool. That's the register split that makes §05h real. We pilot the effortful shape on High Plank first, prove it, then design the calm register as a config variant.

06 Reality check

Wiring risks — resolved by the architecture decisions

The three risks the proposal flagged are all resolved by the rulings, and the fix for each is now an ordered step in docs/scratch/hold-voice-impl-spec.md. ✔ R1 — RESOLVED: hold-episode bookkeeping, not per-second (spec §3)

The generic adapter drives everything off a rising rep count. For a hold, repCount is earned seconds (§02), so this repIncreased branch runs every clean second:

lib/voice/policy_voice_coach.dart — the per-frame diff loopL282–292
final repIncreased = repCount > _lastRepCount;
if (repIncreased) {
  _drainLiveFaults(exercise: exercise, repNumber: _trackedRepNumber);

Resolution (spec §3, simplified further by the 07-12 plank model): under reps-of-holds, repCount increments once per completed hold, not per earned second — so this branch fires exactly at hold completions and the metronome risk dissolves at the source. The rep path becomes the spine (count registration + per-hold outcome off a real RepLog); the adapter's new work shrinks to a within-hold layer (milestones from liveHoldSeconds vs the per-hold target, the milestone switch, earcons, the re-hold push). The fault bookkeeping unit is the hold, which IS the rep — _startTrackingRep scopes correctly with no per-second wipe. Fully specced.

09 Decisions

Forks — how they were ruled

Four choices Nam ruled on in the 2026-07-11 review. The fork framing is kept as history; each now carries its RULED verdict. The options that were weighed stay below for the record, but the decision is closed.

1

The final-countdown stall

The situation: the countdown runs on earned seconds. So if the user breaks form mid-countdown (say at ba), the clock pauses and the countdown stalls there until they fix it — it can't keep ticking to một on a wall clock, because they haven't earned those seconds.

Recommend — honest stall
Let it stall. Resume from the actual earned second when the hold re-commits. The stall is the feedback — same logic as the activation countdown restarting on a broken hold. Keeps the strongest finish-line pull the research found.
Alternative — no per-second countdown
Drop the per-second countdown; announce only còn 5 giây once. Simpler, no stall to reason about — but loses the near-universal numeric countdown that apps ship on-by-default.

✔ RULED — neither: no spoken countdown at all

Nam ruled OUT a spoken per-second countdown entirely. The end-of-hold countdown is non-verbal earcons (§04) — 3 beeps + an end tone — which ride earned seconds, so they stall honestly with no half-spoken ba… freezing mid-word. The stall problem dissolves, and under the two-ring ruling the clock barely stalls mid-hold anyway. A one-line swap to spoken count_1..3 stays available if device feel disagrees.

2

Count direction — voice says "remaining", UI ring counts up

The situation: the voice speaks remaining time (còn 10 giây), but the on-screen ring shows earned seconds counting up (repCount = earned seconds). Those two directions disagree.

Precedent: this exact mismatch already exists and you kept it — the activation ring shows remaining-seconds counting down while the voice counts một, hai, ba up. Your rationale: mid-hold, the user physically can't see the screen, so the voice wins and the ring is secondary.

Recommend — voice says remaining
Keep the voice on "remaining" (matches the finish-line mental model + the countdown), leave the UI ring counting up as-is. Consistent with the activation-ring precedent you already ruled on.
Alternative — align them
Make one follow the other. Removes the mismatch, but forces a change to a UI you already deliberately left alone.

✔ RULED — voice says remaining; UI ring stays exactly as-is

Kept the mismatch on purpose: the coach speaks REMAINING (finish-line mental model), the screen counts however it counts today. Coach = person, screen = tool; mid-hold the user can't see the screen, so the voice wins. No UI change in scope (impl fence: UI ring untouched).

3

Resume-confirm after a fixed break

The situation: after a break is fixed (dropping → holding), should the coach speak a "clock restarted" confirm — something like Oke, đếm tiếp?

Recommend — NO confirm
Stay silent on re-hold. Hustle may fire there anyway (final third), and the next milestone confirms progress soon enough. A deterministic confirm on every re-hold gets naggy on a wobbly holder who breaks and fixes repeatedly.
Counter-argument — the one trust leak
This is the single spot where trust could leak: after a fix, the user can't tell the clock actually restarted (they can't see the ring). A short confirm would close that gap. The cost is nagginess on a shaky hold.

✔ RULED — NO confirm

Stay silent on re-hold. Under the two-ring ruling a re-hold is now rare (only after a real out-of-pose break, not a form fault), so the nagginess worry mostly evaporates; the next milestone and any hustle push confirm progress soon enough. No deterministic "đếm tiếp" line.

4

Milestone set for longer targets (60–90s)

The situation: the research says holds >60s want a check-in roughly every 30s. Do we add that cadence for longer targets, or keep one relative rule at every length?

Recommend — one relative rule
Halfway + final-10 + final-5-countdown, computed relative to the target at any length. No new recordings per duration; the same milestones just land at different absolute times.
Alternative — add ~30s check-ins
For 60–90s holds, insert the research's periodic check-in. More faithful to the long-hold evidence, but needs new "keep going" recordings and re-introduces a near-periodic cue on long holds (mild metronome risk).

✔ RULED — one relative rule at any duration · refined 07-12

Halfway + còn 10 giây (at T−10) + end earcons, computed relative to the target at any length. Zero new audio per duration, no per-duration config, dodges the metronome risk. 07-12 refinement (plank model): T is the per-hold duration, and the milestones live inside each hold — halfway always; còn 10 giây only when the hold is long enough for it to be distinct (a 15s hold keeps halfway only); final-3 beeps always. Long "holds" now arrive as more/longer holds per set rather than one long hold, which also defuses the 60–90s check-in question for the pilot.
